## Why a live successor after predecessor destruction is still blocked

The SDK's `RetainedIdentityHistoryStream.ExcludedSequences` means excluded profile/non-attribution positions. It contains no transition kind, binding revision, immutable boundary, policy-bound expiry or authenticated irreversible-destruction receipt. `IIdentityHistoryCustody.UnprotectEventAsync` returns readable bytes or fails; `CanReadAsync`/`DestroyExpiredAsync` are booleans and do not expose an authenticated exact expired-transition outcome. The Parties fold requires the complete monotonic lifecycle and rejects a successor without its predecessor version proof. Reclassifying an expired binding as a profile exclusion would change the accepted certificate meaning and remove that proof.

A safe owning extension therefore needs a separately versioned, authenticated actor-free continuation/checkpoint or expired-transition certificate, bound to exact tenant/Party, original source position/head, transition kind/version and immutable boundary, accepted policy and fresh nonrollback custody/destruction outcome. It must carry no expired ActorId, private profile, login mapping or recoverable relationship. EventStore owns the source partition/transport and complete authoritative check; Platform custody owns exact lifecycle/irreversible receipts; Parties owns strict version/boundary folding and current/rebind replay. Its allowed data lifetime and compatibility semantics require an explicit owner-approved contract; this packet proposes the required shape without publishing an invented API or policy decision. Current and command replay also need qualified reconstruction without expired predecessor decryption, not merely an attribution-query bypass.

Until that extension is accepted, implemented and qualified, missing predecessor proof remains unavailable. The continuation did not weaken history completeness, retain an expired actor longer, substitute today's binding, or manufacture a tombstone/provider result.

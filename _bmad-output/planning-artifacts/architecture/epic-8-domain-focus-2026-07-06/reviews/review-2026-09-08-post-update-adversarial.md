### Q1 — I16's stop fires on a *marker*; the live Builds bump wrote none

- Severity: **high** (live: HEAD `bf8daf98` moved `references/Hexalith.Builds` to `a32cb422` past the authorized `35c3d1e5`)
- Units: A = any Builds-relying deletion slice (e.g. `8.8-runtime-boundary-cleanup` build-props adoption deleting `Directory.Build.props` probes, or DW-104 deleting `Hexalith.Parties.Security` MOVE files under a package-mode graph whose versions the Builds catalog selects) vs B = the reviewer gate reading the I4 row ("under I16 this is a stop")

The changing commit did neither and has merged. I16 gives no rule for that state; it describes an obligation the commit skipped.
  - **I16 sentence 4:** "An unvalidated marker is a stop, not a state: while any parity claim on a dependency stands unvalidated, no further deletion relying on that dependency may merge." Unit A: no matrix row carries an `unvalidated` marker for Builds (the I4 row confirms: "no signoff, test-constant, or matrix `unvalidated` marker"), so no claim "stands unvalidated"; the stop clause's predicate is false. Unit A's deletion merges with its own fresh parity stamp at `a32cb422` — I16 sentence 1 satisfied at that identity.
  - **I16 sentence 3:** "No deletion authorized by parity evidence may merge into a tree whose retained identity differs from the evidence's stamp." Unit A stamps its new evidence at `a32cb422`; tree and stamp agree.
  - Unit B reads "re-opens" as "unvalidated until re-run" and the I4 row's prose as binding.
- Incompatible outcome: two auditors, one text, opposite decisions on whether any Epic 8 deletion may merge today. The I4 row and frontmatter say "stop"; the invariant's trigger is a marker that only a compliant changing story writes, so a *non-compliant* identity change (exactly the case at HEAD) is the one case the stop does not reach. Every package-mode parity claim relies on the Builds catalog as version authority, so this is not a corner: it is the whole receipt set.
- Close-with (I16, append): "A re-opened claim is unvalidated from the instant the identity changes, whether or not a marker was written; the marker records the state, it does not create it. An identity change that merges without re-run or marker is itself an I16 violation: the next spec touching the tree must record the missing markers (or restore the identity) before any other change, and no deletion may merge meanwhile. The §7 I4 row prints only stamped identities; an unstamped HEAD is a stop regardless of what any row says."

  - **I11:** "allocation of an aggregate ID that is tombstoned under I10 is rejected at the command handler." I10 locates the tombstone in "sequence/checkpoint state" — projection state. Unit A's handler reads the index tombstone: correct layer, letter-compliant.

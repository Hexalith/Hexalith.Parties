**Recommendation: no-go for production implementation of the proposed Luna pair/custom Functionality Module design.** It has no documented end-to-end guarantee for autonomous per-unit destruction through power/clock failure, and its ordinary application plaintext path admits a concrete paused-process counterexample. No supportable non-AWS alternative meeting the complete unchanged contract was verified. This is a feasibility decision, not another delivery-blocker inventory. A capped feasibility investigation is the only recommended next technical expenditure; it must be allowed to reject the backend before provider integration or production hardware purchase.

Branch B and immutable tenant/Party identity remain intact. History expiry remains original binding-effective-at + **31,536,000 seconds**, exactly 365 fixed 24-hour days, with release only while `now < ExpiresAt`. Successor binding, revocation, rotation, copying, retry, erasure and restore cannot renew a predecessor. The complete EXT-PARTIES-1 identity/currentness/authorization/P-01–P-10 scope remains required. Project documents define these requirements; external documentation below is the capability evidence. See [contract extraction](imports/contract-extract.md).

| Required guarantee | Feasibility verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Irreversible destruction of the original unit and every decrypting generation/copy at exclusive expiry, including outages/power loss/backups | **Not established.** Custom code and clocks exist, but none of the reviewed vendor sources commits to the necessary autonomous selective zeroization behavior across the proposed failure model. Standard key-date policy is insufficient. [1][2][3][4][5] |
| Expiry of plaintext and DEKs already in paused/crashed applications | **Fails for an ordinary application-owned cleanup mechanism.** A stopped process cannot execute its cleanup; system sleep can preserve memory without userspace running. Root deletion does not rewrite an independent plaintext copy. [6][7][8] |
| Preservation of unrelated live units through expiry, maintenance and restore | **Not established end to end.** Per-unit roots/segments are sensible engineering, but pair-wide root loss, destructive HSM configuration, maintenance and usable backup recovery remain unresolved. Key survival also does not establish F1 successor query continuity. [1][2][4] |

Confidence is high in the no-go recommendation for this proposal and fault model. Individual vendor capabilities remain single-primary-source observations unless independently corroborated; none is physical qualification of an installed backend. The conclusion does not claim all possible future hardware designs are impossible.

**What the Luna documentation establishes.** Thales documents persistent tamper-protected SMFS, ordinary FM RAM, custom key lifecycle responsibility and timing APIs. The card battery preserves cryptographic material through host power loss. Standard partition policy 39 concerns key-date enforcement for encrypt/sign/wrap; it does not establish timed destruction or this contract's decrypt/unwrap and all-copy semantics. FM execution is programmable, but the evidence does not specify continued timed-erasure execution in every unpowered state. [1][2][3][5]

Those facts expose two conditional design risks. If the proposed FM memory loses an indispensable root before expiry, every live unit needing it becomes unrecoverable. The exact power/reset behavior of that memory is unverified; the SDK RAM listing alone does not establish it. Keeping material in battery-backed storage preserves it through host power loss, but supplies no documented autonomous deletion guarantee at each unit's deadline. Boot-time purge, denied release under uncertain time, or a cleanup receipt issued after connectivity returns cannot prove destruction at the original instant. This engineering inference depends on memory-loss behavior that must be confirmed for the selected device and firmware; neither proposed construction has vendor-certified deadline behavior here. [1][2]

The proposed two-device double wrap, authenticated device-to-device processing, hardware-only AEAD, no root backups and immutable per-generation deadlines are **proposed engineering**. For this two-of-two construction, either device's lost indispensable root makes its cohort unrecoverable; the pair is a dependency, not redundant recovery. Ordinary ciphertext/envelope backups recover live data only while the required original roots survive. The all-copy destruction proof must cover every historical generation, clone, intermediate, service/debug recovery path and alternate wrapping path. A retained restorable root or independent DEK defeats that destruction argument.

Thales also documents destructive FM-enablement configuration and special FM distribution constraints; native client HA cannot be assumed to manage custom FM calls. The proposed replacement-pair drain can avoid intentional live-root migration, but needs capacity and a period as long as the remaining original unit lifetime. It does not solve an urgent firmware/security repair, device failure or reboot that destroys live roots. [4] Dedicated pairs and snapshot segments might preserve unrelated units during *selective* expiry; device-wide reset or a shared snapshot key cannot substitute for it. The vendor rollback page and FM constraints page also differ in wording about the permitted rollback set; neither establishes immutable custom-FM images. Require a pinned vendor answer rather than choosing the favorable interpretation. [4][17]

**Plaintext is the independent disqualifier.** Suppose an authorized application receives actor plaintext just before expiry and is stopped until after it. Linux permits an uncatchable process stop, and its sleep states can retain RAM or persist a hibernation image while userspace does not execute. Cleanup on resume is late. The counterexample still applies when DEKs never leave the HSM: plaintext has already crossed the boundary. [6][7]

Microsoft documents immutable strings and extra plaintext-copy/lifetime obligations. Explicitly zeroing one owned span clears that span; it supplies no guarantee about other strings, serializer/transport buffers, registers, dumps or previously persisted memory images. Nonpageable and non-dumpable buffers reduce particular copy paths; they provide no independent deadline trigger and cannot erase an earlier image. A documented FailFast termination path bypasses finally blocks and finalizers. [8][9][10][19] An external supervisor can add enforcement while it remains operational, but killing a process alone is not evidence of erasing physical memory or existing dumps, and a supervisor paused with the VM does not close the fault case.

A viable redesign would have to keep all in-scope plaintext processing and outputs inside an independently enforceable boundary whose memory actually becomes irrecoverable by the original deadline through the stated failure model. It must include both owning hosts and every SDK/consumer holder. Restricting scope to future reads or to managed buffers would change the guarantee and is not authorized here. TEE attestation or key-release policy proves neither asynchronous erasure of already-released material nor survival of unrelated live units. No such complete boundary was verified in this run. [6][8][13]

**Backups and authority require separate proof.** OpenBao documents restoring earlier Raft state and restoring backed-up Transit key versions. Microsoft documents selective recovery of a purged Managed HSM key and all versions from backup. These independent primary publishers corroborate why deleting a current object cannot establish destruction of a restorable copy. The existing generic OpenBao installation cannot be promoted to custody by adding an expiry check. [12][15][16]

A separately operated quorum, signed receipts and checkpoints are useful proposed components. They do not prove currentness if every witness can be rolled back. Q2 must identify the surviving monotonic witness, trusted time, permitted clock correction and lost-witness behavior outside every data/key/authority restore cut. A clock-uncertainty refusal protects release; it does not destroy material. Q3 must close all C01–C16 inventories and in-flight copy obligations before a final original receipt. Q5 must preserve the original binding lookup when revoking/rebinding. F1 must establish successor completeness without retaining expired actor/provenance/logical references or recoverable hashes. These are engineering requirements, not vendor capabilities.

**What must be obtained before this candidate can be reconsidered.** Written vendor confirmation and physical qualification are different evidence levels. Neither substitutes for the other.

| Area | Written vendor confirmation still needed | Physical qualification still required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power, memory and time | Exact device/firmware/FM memory and CPU/RTC power domains; independent execution or irreversible erasure when all external power is absent; bounded drift/clock-failure behavior; immutable original deadline and nonrollback firmware/time. A promise to purge on a later boot does not meet the requirement. | Power/battery/clock/reset faults around a unit's original expiry, disconnected workloads and authority; measure actual erasure boundary and recovery attempts. Keep a later-expiring independent control on the same relevant hardware. |
| All key paths | Supported selective zeroization and remanence assurance; generations/clones/backups/SMFS/service modes; no derivation/reconstruction bypass; supported two-device protocol and in-boundary AEAD. | Restore genuine pre-expiry data/key/lifecycle cuts; attempt each historical generation and stale/offline device path. Verify expired units cannot recover while live controls recover. |
| Plaintext holders | A supported enforcement boundary covering every recipient, transient copy and memory-image path, even if the application/VM stops or crashes; its time and destruction semantics. | Stop after plaintext receipt, cross expiry, resume and independently examine retained-copy outcomes; crash/dump/hibernate/VM snapshot/power cases and transport buffers. Omitted paths must be authenticated absences, not assumed absences. |
| Isolation and operations | Selective expiry semantics and supported maintenance/update behavior, actual capacity and failure effects. | Mixed-deadline units/snapshots, simultaneous expiry, urgent maintenance, lost acknowledgements, in-flight copy races and positive successor restore/query controls after approved F1. |

There is no authorized late-destruction grace period. Exact physical timing/measurement uncertainty must be reconciled against the existing exclusive deadline; it cannot silently become +100 ms or +5 seconds. Testing can falsify mechanisms and support a bounded assurance case; finite passing tests alone cannot prove a universal physical guarantee. B07–B13 therefore need a documented failure model plus independent evidence on the exact devices/firmware/FM/OS/runtime. Local tests cannot establish Available.

**Non-AWS alternative verdict: none verified.** Azure Managed HSM with Secure Key Release is the strongest examined candidate, but Microsoft explicitly allows decrypt/release/unwrap/verify after key expiration, allows a purged key's versions to be restored from backup, and describes release into an attested execution environment rather than revocation of existing copies. Immutable release policies are a useful access-policy property, not timed destruction. [11][12][13][18] Google distinguishes customer-unrecoverable logical destruction from delayed infrastructure/backup deletion, so its documentation also does not establish the requested all-copy guarantee at the original instant. [14] These are bounded findings about examined mechanisms; no unsupported replacement is recommended.

The smallest practical owner handoff is **five decisions**, grouped by the gate they control. These are recommendations, not questions requiring a response before this research finishes, and none records approval or fills the original ledger.

1. **Fund or stop feasibility.** The project owner and Platform custody/key owner should authorize only a capped, timeboxed vendor-supported feasibility engagement and independent laboratory work, or stop this candidate. Set the spend cap, named engineering allocation and verdict date. Start with the proposal's isolated maximum of 100 live synthetic units/two generations and a loaned/rented pair if supportable; require a supported plaintext-holder mechanism as well as powered/unpowered selective erasure before any provider integration or production purchase. Reject the candidate if either lacks a supported mechanism. **Q1, Q4, Q6; D04; B07, B09, B13.** The proposal's November 13 feasibility checkpoint may be adopted explicitly; it is not accepted now.
2. **Approve exact resources and independent authority, conditional on feasibility.** Platform deployment/custody, Tenants, EventStore and recovery owners must decide whether to reserve the proposed eight environment-separated HSMs (one active and one replacement pair in staging and production), three external authority nodes, two isolated app/data environment pairs, two approved tenants, separate restore target and observer/fault resources. Approve exact device/firmware/FM/signing and cluster/namespace/registry/DNS/TLS identities, quotas and measured capacity before binding them. Q2 additionally requires the actual independent monotonic/time witness outside all restore cuts and its fence/reissue procedure; three replicas or signed old checkpoints are insufficient. **D01, D03; Q1, Q2, Q6; B01–B04, B06–B08, B11, B12.** Resource reservations confer no deployment authority.
3. **Name operators and separate authorities.** Identity/secret operations and copy/recovery owners must name real primary/deputy/escalation/renewal principals for the eleven roles: provisioner, identity writer, reader, observer, custody provider, lifecycle/fence writer, destruction operator, backup operator, restore operator, fault injector and renewer. Specify credential references and only-writer/revocation policies; separate provider, independent authority and independent observer powers, including FM signing. Assign C01–C16 owners and durable work/receipt/recovery hooks; decide linkability/minimization and retention instead of assuming the proposed 400-day receipts are harmless. **D02; Q2–Q4, Q7; D03, D04; B05, B06, B08–B13.** No credentials need be retrieved for this decision.
4. **Accept or reject the availability/data-loss and maintenance envelope.** Operations, key, privacy and recovery owners must explicitly accept or reject the risk of permanently losing the pair's entire still-live cohort, with no root recovery, if either indispensable volatile-root set is lost. Approve or reject drain-until-last-original-expiry, urgent-patch handling, permitted restart/outage classes, quarantine behavior and positive live-unit restore requirements. A design that needs ordinary root backups or routine live-unit destruction fails these constraints unless a separately proven mechanism solves it. Approve proposed retry/currentness/clock/alert/receipt bounds separately from hard expiry; specify exact isolated backup cuts, fault UIDs, abort/disposal bounds and authorized observers. **Q1, Q4, Q7; D03, D04; B07, B08, B10–B13.** Accepting outage/data-loss exposure cannot waive unrelated-unit preservation during normal expiry, maintenance or restore, or permit late destruction.
5. **Approve the complete implementation scope and replace the delivery date only after feasibility passes.** Parties, EventStore, Platform, privacy and Product owners must ratify original-unit collision/revocation mapping and authenticated predecessor lookup for establish/rebind/revoke (Q5), both actual host/package/bootstrap/persistence/query paths and every plaintext holder (Q6), erasure/receipt boundaries (Q7), and the actor-free F1 schema/producer/verifier with positive successor control. Then accept one complete source/package/image/device/FM/install/trust target, a real B15 pass path covering P-01–P-10 plus custody/restore/fault evidence, and an achievable explicit integration date. **Q5–Q7, F1, F2; D01–D04 and Q1–Q4 as prerequisites; B01–B15, especially B03, B09, B14, B15.** The proposed December 14 date is not supported as a commitment today; keep the historical missed October 8 acceptance intact and the replacement accepted date unset until these gates are met.

The next work is owner selection of decision 1. Production implementation is premature. Existing identity/contract work may remain preserved, with custody unavailable. Nothing in this recommendation narrows Branch B or reopens the settled duration/trigger.
